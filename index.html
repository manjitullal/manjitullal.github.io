Publications

When Does Explicit Interaction Feature Engineering Help Gradient-Boosted Trees? A Main-Effect Analysis and Empirical Characterization
Working paper · in preparation for 2027 conference submission

A greedy tree split sees an interaction only through its main effect, so pure interactions like x1x2 are invisible to the first split of every tree. On a 34-task benchmark, generic products and ratios give no significant gain across LightGBM, CatBoost, and XGBoost; the gains that do appear come from features a tree cannot build from a single row, such as frequency encodings and within-group ranks.

Neural Point Process for Learning Spatiotemporal Event Dynamics
L4DC 2022, PMLR 168 · Zhou, Yang, Rossi, Zhao, Yu · contributed research

Contributed RNN and attention baselines for modeling when and where events occur, as a graduate research associate at Northeastern.

Research Projects

Recommenders: matrix factorization to neural collaborative filtering
recommendation · embeddings · PyTorch

On MovieLens, built matrix factorization, matrix factorization with user and item biases, and a neural collaborative-filtering model in PyTorch, alongside a content-based recommender using TF-IDF over movie metadata and a vote-weighted rating.

DE-GAN: document enhancement with GANs
paper reimplementation · GANs · PyTorch

Reimplemented DE-GAN in PyTorch for three tasks: deblurring, cleaning degraded scans, and removing watermarks, each with a standard and a Wasserstein GAN variant. Built a patch-based dataset to stretch 100 source images, and compared PSNR against the paper.

Lung disease detection from chest X-rays
computer vision · medical imaging · PyTorch

15-label disease classification on the NIH chest X-ray dataset with ResNet-101 and asymmetric loss for the heavy label imbalance, evaluated with mAP and ROC curves; and a U-Net written from scratch for lung segmentation.

Classic ML from scratch
NumPy · first principles

Linear regression by least squares and by gradient descent, and logistic regression with hand-written cost and gradient, alongside notebooks on PCA, LDA, and XGBoost.

Transformers for spatiotemporal event prediction
point processes · transformers · PyTorch

Adapted the Transformer Hawkes Process to model when and where the next event happens, with data pipelines for earthquakes, Foursquare check-ins, and NYC event data, plus a sequence-to-sequence Transformer for numeric sequence prediction. Grew out of my graduate research on neural point processes (see Publications).
